Elevation has two parts: a shadow that shows a surface sits above the page, and a z-index band
that decides what actually covers what. A modal uses both, `shadow-ws-high` and `z-ws-modal`.

## Shadows

`shadow-ws-low`, `shadow-ws-medium` and `shadow-ws-high` are the general levels: low for small
controls and cards, medium for dropdowns, cards and navigation, high for modals and popovers.
The others belong to one kind of component: buttons, form fields, the focus ring of an active
field, a frozen table column.

```tsx
<div className="rounded-ws-container-md bg-ws-static-container shadow-ws-medium">…</div>
```

In dark mode most shadows are switched off or reduced to a 1px outline, because a dark
shadow on a dark page does not read. Each class below is drawn on a light and a dark panel.

<ShadowSamples />

To change a shadow, override its `--ws-elevation-*` variable in your stylesheet, under `.dark`
for the dark value. The three without one (`forms-active`, `forms-active-danger`, `focus-base`)
are compiled into the class; they take their colour from the dynamic and static colour tokens,
so they follow a re-brand.

`drop-shadow-ws-*` are `filter` shadows for shapes a box shadow cannot follow, such as the
tooltip with its arrow.

## Z-index

Stacking uses named bands instead of numbers: `z-ws-modal`, `z-ws-sticky`. Components own the
upper bands (drawer, modal, popout, notification), so a tooltip opened inside a modal always
clears it. The lower bands are for your page layout.

```tsx
<header className="sticky top-0 z-ws-sticky">…</header>
```

Each value is a range, not one step. Something that has to sit just above a band can add to it,
as long as it stays below the next one:

```tsx
<div className="z-[calc(var(--ws-z-index-sticky)+1)]" />
```

`z-10`, `z-50` and the rest are Tailwind's own scale and do not line up with these bands.
